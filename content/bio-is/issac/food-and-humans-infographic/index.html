Food and Humans

Ch5 · BB02 Molecules of Life

Organic molecules contain C–C or C–H bonds. Carbon dioxide is not organic.

Seven nutrient groups: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, dietary fibre, vitamins, minerals and ions, and water. Nucleic acids carry genetic information. They are not an energy food.

Key idea

  • Seven nutrient groups fuel, build, and protect the body; nucleic acids carry information but are not energy food.
  • Condensation builds molecules and loses water; hydrolysis breaks them down and gains water.
  • Too little or too much of a nutrient causes disease; food tests show positive results as colour changes or spots.
  • A balanced diet matches energy in with energy out for your age, activity, and life stage.

01

Hydrolysis vs condensation

Types

Condensation

Build-up. Lose water.

Glycosidic bond
Glucose + glucose − H₂O → maltose.
C₆H₁₂O₆ + C₆H₁₂O₆ − H₂O → C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁.

Hydrolysis

Breakdown. Gain water.

Examples
Maltose → glucose + glucose.
Starch can be hydrolysed to maltose.

Lipid

C, H and O. Some also contain P. H:O is greater than 2:1, so there is much more hydrogen than in a carbohydrate.

About 37 kJ/g. Double a lipid result before adding it to carbohydrate or protein energy.

Protein

C, H, O and N. Some also contain S. About 17 kJ/g.

A whole protein is not absorbed. Hydrolysis releases amino acids, which enter capillary blood.

Denaturation is irreversible.

Types

Amino acid

20 types, 20 side chains

Parts
Amino group, which contains N.
Carboxyl group.
Side chain, R. The side chain makes the type.
Essential
Only from food. All 20 types matter.
A vegetarian diet can miss some of the 9 essential amino acids. Those proteins are incomplete.
Non-essential
The body can make them.

From dipeptide to protein

  1. Dipeptide. Two amino acids join by a peptide bond. One water leaves.
  2. Polypeptide. A chain. Inactive until it folds. DNA sets the order.
  3. Protein. The chain folds into a 3D shape. Hydrogen bonds hold it. The order decides the shape and the job. Heat or the wrong pH undoes the shape. That is irreversible.

Jobs

Membrane proteins

Channels, receptors, carriers, antigens

Role

Enzymes

Catalysts

Role
Speed reactions.

Hormones

Chemical signals

Role
Bind a receptor and cause a response.

Antibodies

Immune defence

Role
Antibodies and antigens are proteins.
Antibiotics are not proteins.

Food test

A positive result is a colour change, or a spot that appears and then disappears.

Tests

Glucose

Clinistix

How
  1. Dip the paper in the sample.
Positive
Pink to purple.

Reducing sugar

Benedict’s test

How
  1. Add an equal volume of Benedict’s solution, for example 5 cm³.
  2. Boil, or use a hot water bath, for about 5 minutes.
  3. Look for a precipitate.
Positive
Brick-red precipitate. More precipitate means more reducing sugar.
Green, yellow, or orange-red means less.
The test does not name which reducing sugar it is.
The sugar needs at least one glucose. Sucrose is non-reducing, so it does not give this result.

Starch

Iodine test

How
  1. Boil the leaf to destroy the cell membrane.
  2. Hot alcohol removes chlorophyll.
  3. Wash with water to remove the alcohol.
  4. Then add iodine solution.
Positive
Brown to blue-black.

Protein paper

Albustix

How
  1. Dip the paper in the sample.
Positive
Yellow to blue-green.

Protein solution

Biuret test

How
  1. Add Biuret reagent: sodium hydroxide plus copper sulfate.
  2. Shake gently.
Positive
Blue to violet.

Lipid

Grease-spot test

How
  1. Drop the sample on filter paper.
  2. Look for a translucent spot in the light.
  3. Then drip on an organic solvent such as alcohol.
Positive
A translucent spot that disappears in the solvent.
No spot means no lipid.

Vitamin C

DCPIP test

How
  1. Add the sample drop by drop to DCPIP.
  2. Mix, and watch the colour.
Positive
Blue to colourless.
Fewer drops means a higher vitamin C concentration.
Heat
Heat destroys vitamin C.
Hot lemon tea needs more drops than cold lemon tea to decolourise the same volume of DCPIP.
Comparing
The same amount of vitamin C decolourises the same volume of DCPIP.
If the DCPIP volumes differ, scale the drop count to the same volume before comparing.

Energy

Burning test

How
  1. How long the flame lasts.
  2. Temperature change: final water temperature minus the start.
  3. A bigger temperature rise in a fixed time means more energy.
Lipids
Double the lipid value before you add it to carbohydrate or protein.
Energy per gram is about twice as high.
Errors
Heat lost to the surroundings is not all used to heat the water.
Incomplete combustion leaves chemical energy in the food.
Both errors underestimate the energy.
13

Who needs more

A balanced diet matches energy input from food with energy output from activity.

If input is greater than output, the body stores the extra as fat. Biomass rises, so weight rises.

If output is greater than input, the body breaks down stores such as glycogen and fat. Biomass falls, so weight falls.

Groups

Child

  • Energy. A larger surface-area-to-volume ratio means more heat loss.
  • Protein, for rapid growth such as muscle.

Foetus and pregnancy

  • Protein, to form muscle and tissues.
  • Calcium and vitamin D, to grow bones and teeth.
  • Iron, for blood and haemoglobin.
  • Dietary fibre in pregnancy, to prevent constipation.

Elderly

  • More calcium and vitamin D, to prevent osteoporosis.
  • Less energy if less active.

Office worker

  • Less energy. The work is sedentary.
  • More dietary fibre and water, to prevent constipation.

Physical worker

  • Energy, to support muscle movement.

Indoor life

  • Vitamin D. There is not enough sunlight.

Male

  • Energy and protein. More muscle.
  • Less subcutaneous fat, so more heat is lost.

Female at puberty

  • Iron, because of blood loss in menstruation.

Why it matters

  • Food tests give quick evidence for glucose, starch, protein, lipid, and vitamin C in samples—you use colour and spot changes, not guesswork.
  • Knowing kJ/g and doubling lipids in the burning test helps you compare energy fairly in prac write-ups.
  • Deficiency and excess links diet to real symptoms; matching groups (child, pregnancy, elderly) to needs supports a balanced diet argument in exams.
