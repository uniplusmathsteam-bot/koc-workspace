Parallel lines keep the same tilt

parallel \(\iff m_1=m_2\)

The vertical gap never closes, so the lines never meet. Two vertical lines are parallel too — both slopes are undefined, and that still counts.

A quarter turn makes a perpendicular

Run \(1\), rise \(2\) gives \(m=2\). A quarter turn swaps the legs and flips a sign: run \(2\), rise \(-1\), so \(m=-\dfrac{1}{2}\).

\(m_1 m_2=-1\)

Horizontal and vertical are perpendicular, but one slope is undefined. Do not multiply in that case — see it.

Moving a point, as coordinates

\(A(4,-2)\) rotated \(90^\circ\) clockwise about the origin.

\((x,y)\rightarrow(y,-x)\), so \(B=(-2,-4)\). One quarter turn swaps the coordinates and changes a sign.

Slide \(B\) up by \(t\): \(C=(-2,-4+t)\). \(A\), \(O\) and \(C\) are collinear when the slopes from \(O\) match.

\(\dfrac{-4+t}{-2}=\dfrac{-2}{4}\Rightarrow t=5\)

\(180^\circ\): \((x,y)\rightarrow(-x,-y)\). Reflect in the \(x\)-axis: \((x,-y)\). Translate up by \(t\): add \(t\) to \(y\). That is the whole list these questions use.

The midpoint is halfway along both legs

\(A(5,-2)\) to \(B(-11,2)\). Half the run and half the rise land on the average.

\(M=\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2},\,\dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)=(-3,0)\)

If \(M\) is known and one end is known, the other end is the point the same distance past \(M\): \(B=2M-A\).

\(M(-5,12)\), \(A(-3,10)\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(B=(-7,14)\).

Diagonals of a parallelogram share a midpoint. In coordinates, that shared midpoint is the test.

A ratio cuts the segment into shares

\(AM:MB=1:2\) means 1 share of the way from \(A\) toward \(B\), out of 3.

\(AM:MB=m:n \Rightarrow \left(\dfrac{nx_1+mx_2}{m+n},\,\dfrac{ny_1+my_2}{m+n}\right)\)

The midpoint is the special case \(1:1\). If a question gives \(CM:MD\), those two numbers are \(m\) and \(n\). Same formula.

Area is base times height, read off the axes

area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\times\text{base}\times\text{height}\)

On the \(x\)-axis the height is the \(y\)-coordinate. If the base is horizontal but higher up, the height is the difference of the \(y\)-coordinates.

A trapezium with the two parallel sides horizontal: average those two lengths, then multiply by the vertical gap.

The coordinate tests

A perpendicular bisector does both jobs: it passes through the midpoint, and \(m_1 m_2=-1\).

Every point on it is the same distance from the two ends. Equal distance is the same test.
