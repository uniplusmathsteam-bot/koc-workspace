Parallel lines keep the same tilt

parallel means \(m_1=m_2\)

The vertical gap never closes, so the lines never meet. Two vertical lines are parallel too.

A quarter turn makes a perpendicular

The green line climbs 2 for every 1 across, so \(m=2\). Turn that step a quarter turn: 2 across and 1 down, so \(m=-\dfrac{1}{2}\).

\(m_1 m_2=-1\)

A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular, but one slope is undefined. Do not multiply in that case.

Moving a point, as coordinates

\(A(4,-2)\) rotated \(90^\circ\) clockwise about the origin.

\((x,y)\rightarrow(y,-x)\), so \(B=(-2,-4)\). The two coordinates swap, and one sign changes.

Slide \(B\) up by \(t\): \(C=(-2,-4+t)\). \(A\), \(O\) and \(C\) are collinear when the slopes from \(O\) match.

\(\dfrac{-4+t}{-2}=\dfrac{-2}{4}\Rightarrow t=5\)

\(180^\circ\): \((x,y)\rightarrow(-x,-y)\). Reflect in the x-axis: \((x,-y)\). Translate up by \(t\): add \(t\) to \(y\).

The midpoint is halfway in x and in y

\(M=\left(\dfrac{5+(-11)}{2},\dfrac{-2+2}{2}\right)=(-3,0)\)

On the horizontal line the x-values are equally spaced. On the vertical line the y-values are equally spaced. That is why each coordinate is an average.

If \(M\) and one end are known, the other end is the same step past \(M\): \(B=2M-A\). For \(M(-5,12)\) and \(A(-3,10)\), \(B=(-7,14)\).

A ratio cuts the segment into shares

\(AM:MB=1:2\) means 3 equal shares, and \(M\) has finished only 1 of them, so \(M\) is closer to \(A\).

\(AM:MB=m:n \Rightarrow \left(\dfrac{nx_1+mx_2}{m+n},\dfrac{ny_1+my_2}{m+n}\right)\)

The weight is the other share. \(MB=2\) multiplies \(A\), and \(AM=1\) multiplies \(B\). The longer piece gives more weight to the opposite end.

From 0 to 9, \(M=(2\cdot 0+1\cdot 9)/3=3\). That is 1 share of the way. The midpoint is the case \(1:1\). If a question gives \(CM:MD\), those two numbers are \(m\) and \(n\).

Area, read from the coordinates

The base is a difference of x-coordinates: \(|7-1|=6\). The height is a difference of y-coordinates: \(|4-0|=4\).

\(\dfrac{1}{2}\times 6\times 4=12\)

If the horizontal side is not on the x-axis, the height is still the difference of the y-coordinates. A trapezium with horizontal parallel sides uses those two x-differences, then the vertical gap between them.

Which formula each centre needs

Centroid
Midpoint of a side, then the section formula \(2:1\) from the vertex. Or average the three vertices.
Circumcentre
Midpoint of a side, then \(m_1 m_2=-1\) for the perpendicular bisector. Intersect two of those lines.
Orthocentre
Slope of a side, then \(m_1 m_2=-1\). Draw that altitude through the opposite vertex and intersect two altitudes.
Incentre
Distance formula for the side lengths \(a,b,c\). Then \(I=\dfrac{ax_1+bx_2+cx_3}{a+b+c}\), and the same for \(y\).

Collinear is the equal-slope test. A perpendicular bisector is the midpoint together with \(m_1 m_2=-1\).
