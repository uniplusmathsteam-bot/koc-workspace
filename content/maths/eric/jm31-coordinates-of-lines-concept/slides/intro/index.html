Distance and slope

Two points determine a right triangle. The coordinates of those points are the only ingredients.

A(x₁, y₁) B(x₂, y₂)

Where each coordinate goes

A(x₁, y₁) B(x₂, y₂) x₁ x₂ y₁ y₂ x₂ − x₁ y₂ − y₁

x₁ and x₂ move onto the horizontal side. y₁ and y₂ move onto the vertical side. One pair at a time.

\(AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\)

Put the numbers in, one at a time

\(A(2,11),\ B(-4,3)\)

\(AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\)
\(x_1=2\)
\(y_1=11\)
\(x_2=-4\)
\(y_2=3\)
\(AB=\sqrt{(-4-2)^2+(3-11)^2}\)
\(AB=\sqrt{(-6)^2+(-8)^2}\)
\(AB=\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}\)
\(AB=10\)

Slope uses the same two changes

\(m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)

Change in y over change in x. Uphill is positive. Downhill is negative. The ratio is the same for every pair of points on the line.

Zero, and undefined

A horizontal line has slope 0. A vertical line has undefined slope, because the change in x is 0 and you cannot divide.

On a polygon, sort the sides by looking: uphill, downhill, flat, or straight up and down.

Same slope means collinear

\(m_{AB}=\dfrac{2-(-7)}{3-10}=\dfrac{9}{-7}\)

\(m_{BC}=\dfrac{11-2}{-4-3}=\dfrac{9}{-7}\)

same slope, so A, B and C lie on one line

Intercepts and a point with a slope

Between \((-8,0)\) and \((0,2)\), the change in y is 2 and the change in x is 8.

\(m=\dfrac{2-0}{0-(-8)}=\dfrac{1}{4}\)

The other way: a point and a slope already fix the line. The slope says how y changes when x changes.

Through \((6,2)\) with \(m=-\dfrac{2}{3}\): \(\;y-2=\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)(x-6)\)

\(x=0\) gives \((0,6)\). \(y=0\) gives \((9,0)\).

A point on the y-axis is \((0,q)\). Put it into the same equation and solve for \(q\).

Inclination is the same ratio

\(m=\tan\theta=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)

\(\theta\) is the angle with the positive x-axis. Among uphill lines, steeper means a larger slope. Downhill lines stay negative.
