Distance and slope

Any two points on a straight line sit on the same right triangle.

Drop straight across, then straight up. Both formulas are just that triangle.

Where the distance formula comes from

Pythagoras on those two legs — that is the whole derivation.

\(AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\)

\(A(2,11),\; B(-4,3):\quad 6^2+8^2=100,\quad AB=10\)

Horizontal: the rise is 0, so the length is just \(|x_2-x_1|\). Vertical: just \(|y_2-y_1|\). If \(a^2+b^2=c^2\) on the three sides, the angle is right.

Slope is the same triangle

\(m=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}\)

Uphill is positive. Downhill is negative. The ratio is the same at every pair of points, because those little triangles are copies of each other.

Zero, and undefined

A horizontal line has slope \(0\). A vertical line has undefined slope — the run is \(0\), so you cannot divide.

On a polygon, sort the sides by looking: uphill, downhill, flat, or straight up and down.

Same slope means collinear

\(A(10,-7),\; B(3,2),\; C(-4,11)\)

\(m_{AB}=\dfrac{2-(-7)}{3-10}=\dfrac{9}{-7}\)

\(m_{BC}=\dfrac{11-2}{-4-3}=\dfrac{9}{-7}\)

equal slopes \(\Rightarrow\) \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) are collinear

If the two slopes differ, the path bends. Those three points are not one straight line.

Intercepts and a point with a slope

Between the intercepts \((-8,0)\) and \((0,2)\), rise \(=2\), run \(=8\).

\(m=\dfrac{2-0}{0-(-8)}=\dfrac{1}{4}\)

The other way: a point and a slope already are the line. \(m\) says how \(y\) must change when \(x\) changes.

Through \((6,2)\) with \(m=-\dfrac{2}{3}\): \(\;y-2=\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)(x-6)\)

\(x=0\) hits the \(y\)-axis at \((0,6)\). \(y=0\) hits the \(x\)-axis at \((9,0)\).

A point on the \(y\)-axis is \((0,q)\). Put it into the same slope equation and solve for \(q\).

Inclination is just the slope angle

\(m=\tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}\)

\(\theta\) is the angle with the positive \(x\)-axis. Among uphill lines, steeper means a larger slope. Downhill lines stay negative. No extra tangent work — the ratio you already have is \(\tan\theta\).
